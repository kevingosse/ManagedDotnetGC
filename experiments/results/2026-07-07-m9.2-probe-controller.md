# M9.2: probe-and-anchor boost controller — first real-app throughput win (OrchardCore 1.06× pinned stock-h8)

Fixes the bottleneck measured in [2026-07-07-orchardcore.md](2026-07-07-orchardcore.md)
("The bottleneck, measured"): the M9.1 boost controller's absolute gates (8/16 MB
survivor mass, 10 ms cheap pause — calibrated on TechEmpower's 2.4 MB in-flight
survivors) read OrchardCore backwards. Its ~18 MB of *concurrency-bound* survivors
tripped the 16 MB shrink gate on 661/976 collections and its 12 ms fixed-cost pause
(roots 5.4 + cards 4.3 ms) failed the cheap gate, pinning the budget at 64–137 MB:
10 young/s + 1.6 promoted-fulls/s ≈ 15% of wall stopped.

## The design: grow is a probe, survivors are the verdict

The measured signal that survived every A/B: OrchardCore's marked_mb is **invariant
to budget** (~18–19 MB at 64 MB and at 256 MB) — it is in-flight request state,
bounded by concurrency, not by collection interval. GCPerfSim-shape survivors are
allocation-proportional — they *follow* the budget. So the controller now measures
exactly that derivative instead of any static mass:

- Every grow records the survivor mass that justified it (`_boostSurvivorAnchor`)
  and doubles the boost — the grow *is* the probe.
- Any young collection whose survivors exceed **anchor×1.5 + 8 MB** halves the boost
  and mutes growth for 30 s. Proportional mass doubles with the doubled budget and
  reverts one collection later; invariant mass passes at every level.
- d(survivors)/d(budget) is budget-independent, so the gate has **one fixed point per
  workload** — unlike the M8 ratio gate (self-referential, two stable fixed points)
  and unlike absolute gates (one fixed point, but a per-app calibration that broke on
  the first real app).
- The **cheap-pause gate is gone**: once survivor invariance holds, a bigger budget
  buys the same per-pause cost fewer times — an expensive fixed pause is a reason to
  grow, not to block.
- The **floor bound now reads durable live** (`_liveAtLastFull`, max 79.8 MB on
  Orchard) instead of sticky live (inflated to 191 MB by the very promotion the
  pinned budget causes — the gate was locking out the exact heap it was built for).
  Soak-shape heaps (live 268–641 MB) stay excluded.

Grow cadence (<250 ms), sparse-cadence shrink (>1 s), cap 192 MB, and the demand
arithmetic (`2×budget − boost`) are unchanged from M9.1.

- **+10.6% over the old controller, and the first real-app result above pinned
  server GC (1.055×).** 0.96× DATAS (was 0.91×). p50 now matches stock.
- gcstats (same protocol as the diagnosis run): young 976→**400** (10→~4.4/s),
  fulls 236→**112**, budget 274–354 MB (boost pegged at cap), survivors p50 19 MB
  at a 300 MB budget — **invariance confirmed in production, zero false
  violations**. Young pause p50 12.3→15.5 ms (cards over 315 vs 253 dirty regions
  from the longer interval, sweep over a bigger nursery) but ×2.4 fewer: **STW
  share of wall roughly halved (~20%→~10%)**.
- Episodic p99 spikes (~170 ms) hit every GC this sitting — ours 1/6 iters, h8 2/3,
  DATAS 1/3. The mid-life-tail question vs DATAS (replicated long runs, p99.9)
  stays open, but our tail is the most consistent of the three here.
- **The trade: WS ~1000→~1250 MB** (committed plateaus at 1056). Committed ≈
  budget × young-per-full (300×3.6) — real mid-life state smears survivors across
  every nursery region between fulls, so the fragmentation floor scales with the
  budget on a non-moving heap. 1.34× pinned-h8, 2.7× DATAS: WS is now clearly the
  weak axis on real apps. Levers if we buy it back: total-budget cap, FullRatio
  frontier (more full cadence), or accepting it as the price of the throughput/tail
  position.

  branch + durable-live floor replace it, verified live).
